# Mobile, network, arcade and spin-off catalogue

Research cut-off: **3 November 2026**. This is a public-source design reference,
not a report of binaries played or a certification of complete feature parity.
It deliberately retains obscure named releases whose surviving documentation is
too thin to reconstruct their mechanics. An unknown feature is not absent.

The intended eventual outcome is additive, selectable Neptune profiles and
creator capabilities. The [factory/Saturn priority](../FACTORY_FIRST_GOAL.md),
[Saturn baseline](../SATURN_FIDELITY.md) and
[community-content contract](../COMMUNITY_CONTENT.md) remain in force. A branded
slot machine or cameo is catalogue context, not an instruction to add gambling
or an unrelated genre to the core battle game.

## How to read the evidence

- **Documented:** an inspected source describes the release or feature. This is
  not an independently reproduced observation.
- **Lead:** a directory or search result names a release; date, build identity,
  licensing or mechanics still need confirmation.
- **Proposed:** a possible Neptune capability inspired by a documented mechanic.
  It has no implementation-status implication.
- Years are the earliest release established by the cited evidence, not a claim
  that every carrier, country, resolution or app revision launched together.
- Japanese feature-phone services, Java ME exports and smartphone adaptations
  must be pinned separately. They can have different stage counts, opponents,
  controls, multiplayer, saves and sound.
- Entries below include ports, revisions and cancelled projects. They must not
  be summed as an authoritative count of distinct released games.

The specialist [master list][S01] expressly limits its comprehensive claim to
2008; its [mobile list][S02] expressly warns that the surviving record is incomplete.
Its newer rows also contain errors: for example its original Bombergirl and
R Online years differ from better release evidence. It is a discovery index,
not a universal authority. Fandom sometimes blocked full-page retrieval; such
entries remain leads unless another inspected source supplies the detail.

## Network computer games

### Online Bomberman / Bomberman Online — Windows, 2003 service family

This is the MGAME/Hudson network game for Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Hong Kong and
Macau, distinct from the 2001 Dreamcast game. Surviving secondary evidence
describes regional services and a Japanese relaunch in December 2005. [N01]

- Survival supports individual elimination and team battles.
- Panel Paint uses bomb explosions to paint floor tiles in the player's team
  colour. A defeated participant revives, losing their occupied area.
- Hyperbomb asks players to collect three of the five special items and reach
  the centre; possession, death and item-loss decisions form the objective.
- Dropbomb removes ordinary bomb placement: survive falling bombs and interfere
  using field items. The source says this later changed into Boss Survival.
- Boss Survival/Boss Battle pits players against a selected boss that uses
  special bombs; bought items provide additional effects.
- Regional item shops offered character/nickname cards, clothing and accessories.
  Bomberyen could be earned through play, bosses or tournaments, or purchased.
- Some character purchases were time limited; nickname background and text-colour
  customisation were separate cosmetic options.
- Unused data labels for Submarine Mode and Ring Match are **not evidence of
  released playable modes**.

The source calls the game four-mode while listing historical replacements and
five named rule families. Preserve the time/version distinction instead of
claiming all were available simultaneously. All original regional servers are
reported closed; shutdown dates and player limits need regional primary records.
The commonly repeated six-player PC limit should not be copied to PS2 or every
revision without checking a manual. The secondary article flags weak sourcing.

### Net de Bomberman — PlayStation 2, 2004

Cross-reference [console/modern catalogue](console-modern.md). The inspected
network overview describes the Japanese PS2 adaptation, online-only play,
subscription service, chat, up to eight participants and ten maps. It reports
a free pre-launch test before the February 2004 retail launch and a later
service closure. These are PS2 claims, not Windows feature guarantees. [N01]

### Bomberman Online Japan — Windows, 2008

This successor used the 55Shock! service; it is not merely another name for the
2003 Windows client. Public service began 10 September 2008. [N01]

- Battle experience feeds a bomb-waza bar/gauge and unlocks a bomb technique.
- Items obtained in courses can be retained for later use.
- Launch rewards included named weapons and special bombs; these are promotion
  leads, not a complete permanent equipment inventory.
- Payment/service interruptions and the June 2009 final closure belong in the
  release ledger; no current official matchmaking should be assumed.

## Smartphone and portable-device releases

### Bomberman Touch: The Legend of Mystic Bomb — iOS, 2008

Documented as a solo maze adventure starring Bomber John, with the Ka-Boom
Temple treasure premise. It uses an on-screen left directional control and
right bomb button. The inspected account explicitly says there is no multiplayer
in this first Touch game. Do not confuse it with DS **Land Touch!**. [M01]

Research still needed: stage/secret/boss list, item pool, original versus 2.0
controls, save rules and whether later app revisions changed the campaign.

### Bomberman Touch 2: Volcano Party — iOS, 2009

The contemporary Hudson release announcement, rather than an inferred platform
capability, establishes the following. [M02]

- Bluetooth peer-to-peer play on compatible iPhone/iPod touch with OS 3.0.
- Battle supports four combatants **including two CPU players**; this does not
  establish four human phones.
- Challenge has two-player action and cooperative progression while escaping
  advancing lava, navigating a labyrinth and reaching a boss.
- The announcement advertises the earlier Touch content plus new features.

Separate the maximum human count, total combatants and supported hardware. A
modern reproduction would need explicit lava-scroll timing, camera rules,
separated-player handling, shared failure and boss-transition contracts.

### Bomberman Dojo — Android 2009; iOS by 2011

Do not flatten it to a 2010-only entry: Hudson's March 2009 announcement and a
May 2009 Android review establish the earlier release family. [M03], [M04], [M05]

- Clear a tower of enemy-filled stages and receive a Bobal Rank at the rooftop.
- The iOS review describes fifty puzzling stages, limited lives and scoring for
  performance; the Android review describes being returned to the bottom after
  dying. Exact platform/save differences remain to measure.
- Power-ups include bomb capacity, fire range, speed increases/decreases,
  additional hit points, Bomb Kick and remotely detonated bombs.
- A level editor, online map sharing and downloading user levels are documented.
- The Android announcement offers tap D-pad or flick movement. The iOS review
  describes a left analogue control and separate placement/detonation buttons.
- Both reviews describe a single-player game; map sharing is not multiplayer.

This is a concrete historical precedent for editor-to-download-to-play workflows.
Its existence also cautions against describing older Bomberman games as having
no user content. Online service availability and editor format are unverified.

### Bomberman Chains — iOS, 2011; free-to-play relaunch 2014

The specialist record documents a RucKyGAMES collaboration, fifty stages and
two time attacks; the relaunch is a revision, not an automatically separate
game. A 2015 delisting is reported. [M06]

- Swap adjacent enemies in an 8×8 match-three board.
- Three or more in a line clear; falling replacements allow chains while the
  board settles, increasing score multipliers.
- Clear four or more and Bomberman supplies a bomb into an emptied column.
- Tap bombs to detonate; chains raise a firepower gauge and blast range, while
  inactivity lets the gauge drain.
- Stage objectives can require a particular enemy, bomb-only clears or a minimum
  chain depth; chains replenish the stage timer.
- Thirty-second and sixty-second high-score modes use different enemy variety.
- A hint flashes after inactivity; an unsolvable board without bombs resets.
- The guide describes queued earned bombs and placement depending on Bomberman's
  current column. Queue and board-settle ordering need real-build measurement.

This is a puzzle profile, not the falling-piece Panic Bomber ruleset.

### 100-nin Taisen Bomberman / 100 Man Battle Bomberman — iOS, 2012

The specialist page dates the Japanese release to 28 March 2012 and describes
more than a hundred levels, many CPU Bombermen and a level-up reward system.
The mobile index also names the Android/iOS family. [M07], [S03]

The title does **not** prove one hundred human network players. Secondary
summaries describe a player accompanied by CPU allies against up to one hundred
CPU enemies. Establish waves, actual concurrent actors, local human support,
objectives, item persistence and level rewards in a pinned build.

### Bomberman — Android/iOS, 2014

The lightweight free-to-play smartphone game is documented separately from both
the old feature-phone game and its later Taisen!/VS! successor. [M08], [S04]

- Four-combatant battle, CPU play and local multiplayer are described.
- Bomber Coliseum offers challenges against 25, 50 or 100 foes; the specialist
  explicitly says they do not all appear simultaneously.
- Characters and arenas were unlocked by challenges or payments.

The source's opinion that this was the franchise's *first* microtransaction
game conflicts with its own earlier Taisen/GREE evidence. Retain the specific
shop mechanics; reject that unqualified historical first claim.

### Taisen! Bomberman / VS! Bomberman — smartphone, 2015–2016 family

Regional dates require separate store records: the specialist distinguishes the
2015 title from Taisen ☆, while the broad release list calls VS! a 2016 release.
The later app is described as reusing the 2014 assets with broader play. [M09], [S04]

- A normal single-player campaign adds enemies, traps and progression.
- Online battle has friend rooms and rankings.
- Four touch-control methods are described; the exact alternatives need capture.
- Missions unlock content; Diamonds speed or bypass some progression steps.
- Starting item packs affect battle power and therefore belong in room rules,
  not in the purely cosmetic loadout system.
- The source reports end of service on 26 August 2019, after July notification.

Claims about matchmaking quality are historical user experience, not a measured
network benchmark or proof that all versions shared identical ranking rules.

### Amazing Bomberman — Apple Arcade, 2022–2025

Official sources establish launch on 5 August 2022 across iPhone, iPad, Mac and
Apple TV, and final service termination on **29 May 2025 PDT**. [M10], [M11]

- The contest is highest score during one song, rather than last survivor.
- Track duration sets match duration; bomb placement/explosion sounds vary with
  the song. Musical peaks cause block drops and possible item opportunities.
- Players can revive repeatedly while the music continues.
- The score leader receives a spotlight and awards a bonus when bombed.
- Worldwide online battles and private friend battles via room ID are documented.
- Solo practice, missions and collectible customisation items are documented.
- Seven launch stages were advertised; the final website lists ten music entries.
  Neither number is a verified final stage/variant denominator.

Research targets: authored event chart, deterministic block-drop schedule, track
clock versus simulation clock, respawn safety, score attribution, leader-change
ties, audio latency and an accessible non-audio cue for scheduled hazards.

### Super Bomberman mobile: useful granular distinctions

The inspected English-build guide describes thirty stages, a hidden exit,
one hidden item per stage and a 300-second stage limit. Battle can end after
one, three or five wins and declares a draw on timeout. Six visual arena themes
are named; CPU starting bomb/fire/speed items are configurable. [J02]

Bomb/fire/speed persist until the run ends, whereas other items are lost on a
life loss according to the guide. It also lists kick, remote control, wall pass,
fireproof suit and extra life. Eight enemy patterns are described: slow
straight-line Ballom, junction-turning Onil, block-passing Kondoria, Dall,
Minvo, fast block-passing Ovapi, proximity-following Pass and Pontan.

The guide explicitly labels uncertainty in enemy scoring and in the meaning of
CPU personality names. Record these as experiment questions. Do not turn
"Soldier/Philosopher/Lawyer/..." into proven difficulty tiers.

### Bomberman 3D: carrier differences matter

The 2004 family combines grid movement with raised platforms, ledges, ramps,
springs, alternate exits, bosses and stage completion tracking. The guide
describes double-tap ledge jumps, stun after a long drop, kick/throw over ledges
and five save slots. It records bomb/fire/speed upgrades, speed-down, shields,
life, score bonuses, bomb/wall passing, glove/kick, thrown impact bombs, line
placement, enemy freeze, invulnerability, remote, piercing and bouncing bombs.
These are edition-scoped guide claims, not a universal smartphone item list. [J04]

The smaller Living Mobile build is documented with a reduced VS configuration,
one CPU and one arena. Some normal-game abilities carry into VS. Meanwhile the
preserved original i-Mode release includes content missing from commonly seen
Java ME builds. The September 2025 preservation account says the original has
six worlds and that surviving J2ME content is incomplete. [J05]

The specialist's partial 176×208 guide and the newer preservation report differ
on counted levels/worlds. Resolve by exact build and branch-route inventory;
do not silently combine their totals. FOMA901i "3D sound" is another device
feature, not proof every international phone played the same audio.

### Taisen ☆: social, spectator and capacity features

Across the seven documented battle revisions, feature breadth includes: [J06]

- Group creation/joining and group-specific match settings.
- Scrolling eight-player arenas versus fixed smaller matches.
- Sideline Misobon participation after elimination in selected modes.
- Team survival or most remaining members at timeout.
- Twenty-four-player free-for-all and four teams of six.
- Item scattering on defeat and timed falling-block sudden death.
- Three arena sizes and manually moved spectator camera in selected revisions.
- Cosmetic character variants, menu backgrounds, music and title rewards.
- Staff challenge events, regional rankings and limited themed tournaments.
- Character skins tied to Hudson properties and the rock band RIZE.
- GEEK pre-match equipment, Heart, stationary block camouflage and extended
  revenge blast range; these are gameplay changes, not cosmetic skins.
- GEEK beginner bomb/fire aid with a win/match-count expiry, and restrictions
  preventing some achievement titles from being earned in CPU/friend rooms.
- Cross-carrier multiplayer and optional message suppression in Cross.

These need separate mode/version records. Purchasing power and timed exclusives
are historical mechanics to understand; Neptune's freely selectable compatible
custom content requirement remains an intentional product choice.

### Shared puzzle mechanics to specify

Falling Bomberman-head groups are rotated and matched in lines. Matching creates
bombs; a burning bomb ignites stored bombs, creating chains and sending garbage
to the opponent. Building chains and using a giant bomb clears your own crowded
well while pressuring opponents. Top-out is the loss condition. Exact geometry,
spawn sequence, gravity and garbage timing are edition contracts. [P01], [P04], [P06]

The PC Engine **Road to Boba Temple** adds fifty authored challenges, including
line-clearing and simultaneous-bomb objectives with an ascending timer. This
suggests reusable objective descriptors and per-puzzle completion records. [P01]

### Panic Bomber W distinctions

- Story/password entry plus separate two-, three- and four-player menu options.
- Selectable difficulty, sound type and controller layout.
- Matching may include diagonal lines; do not import this to another puzzle
  edition merely because the art is similar.
- Charge meter and larger matches/chains help earn a Large Burning Bomb.
- Fire-range progression changes which separated bombs can ignite each other.
- Ordinary bombs, burning bombs, bombfaces/garbage, rocks and large bombs have
  different clear/ignition behaviour.
- Boss-specific garbage and random effects can reverse controls, freeze the
  screen, obscure it, reduce range, transform garbage into bombs or change heads.
- Optional encounters and different difficulty passwords are guide-documented;
  exact unlock conditions and all boss attack tables remain unverified. [P04]

The specialist identifies Dokuro/Skull Mode: destructible garbage blocks can
reveal a helpful item or opponent ailment. This named option should be compared
against the guide's less certain account of its menu. [P03]

The 2026 official update confirms W, Big Bomb strategy, BOMB Radio tracks and
the collection's Save/Load Anytime, Rewind, Boss Rush and illustration gallery.
These wrapper features must not be retroactively attributed to the 1995 cart.
Whether each wrapper option applies identically inside W needs direct inspection. [P08]

The preserved Satellaview trial is documented by its preservation researcher as
premiering 1 May 1995, with later rebroadcasts. It only permits story Round 2,
including tutorial/secret opponents, disables multiplayer and difficulty/boss
mode changes, and lacks the retail game's SA-1 requirement. Broadcast-date
uncertainties are explicit in the source. Do not attribute the full retail
mode/content inventory to this trial. [P09]

### Bomberman Bandai Mame Game LCD — circa 1997

A firsthand review identifies this dedicated handheld by packaging copyright,
not a proven exact sale date. It describes a dual-layer LCD and a four-quadrant
continuous playfield with offscreen block/enemy edge cues. [X07]

- Normal play: clear enemies and find the randomly hidden block exit.
- One active small-range bomb, persisting when the player changes quadrant.
- Every three stages, a timing bonus asks the player to kick a bomb into a crab.
- The manual reportedly specifies 99 stages; the reviewer did not play all 99.
- Master Mode offers sixteen selectable one-life time-attack layouts.
- Sound toggle, pause and automatic power-off with resume are documented.

This is useful precedent for concise offscreen-hazard indicators, resumable
short-session play and optional restricted-inventory challenge profiles. It is
not evidence that a native digital replay of the toy has been recovered.

### Bomberman The Medal — arcade, 2018

An inspected specialist record with official/game-press citations describes
an eight-seat shared table game and 2019 content update. [A01]

- Each seat is a Bomberman Brother; players can join an ongoing field.
- Bomb placement consumes medals, with four selectable bomb-power/cost tiers.
- A projected floor indicator shows the placed bomb's expected blast range.
- Bomb contact stuns; monsters can be walked through without damage.
- Monster defeats fill a gauge for item/bonus opportunities.
- Different monsters award medals, scatter bombs, enable roulette or open a
  Special Stage; boss rewards depend on damage contribution.
- The record names Fire Up, Bomb Up, Speed Up, Dynamite, Fire Suit, Silver Bomb
  and Barrage Bomb, plus a hidden conventional battle mode.
- Bridge Town, Duct Plant, Neon Palace, Eldoracrown and Ruin Room are named
  update stages. They are a partial documented list, not total coverage.

The finite-ammunition/resource idea, visible blast preview, shared boss field
and drop-in participation are separable inspirations; medal economics and
gambling reward tables are not default game requirements.

### Bombergirl arcade family and Konasute PC

The central mode is two teams of four attempting to destroy the enemy base.
Characters use bombs plus differing abilities: extra bombs, block construction,
fast movement and ranged attacks are explicitly described. Match levelling
opens special attacks. A roster has gameplay capabilities, not just visual
skins; compatibility and balance rules must express that difference. [A02]

roles; introduces rental use of unowned characters in single battle without
rank gain; renews a play-stamp reward system; increases rank caps; and preserves

are **2022** features, not a claim that all were in the 2018 launch. [A04]

The official PC news archive separately evidences ranked seasons, single
missions, private-match availability windows, rotating maps, costumes/skill
draws and repeated balance updates through October 2026. Its literal role labels
corroborate the four-role design. An older community changelog describes cooldown,
hitbox, invincibility, skill timing and matchmaking changes; it warns its text is
machine-translated and PC balance is usually, not always, equivalent. [A03], [A05]

text to extraction. Full map, base-health, respawn, tower, rush, skill and

MOBA conventions. The current live roster is not independently enumerated here.
