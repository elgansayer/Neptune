# Handheld Bomberman: release identities and feature research

Researched 2026-11-03. This is a public-source design reference and eventual
aspiration list. No cartridge, emulator or hardware was executed for this survey.
The detail below is documented behavior, not measured Neptune parity. Preserve
the [Saturn baseline](../SATURN_FIDELITY.md), [community pack contract](../COMMUNITY_CONTENT.md)
and [factory/Saturn-first delivery priority](../FACTORY_FIRST_GOAL.md).

Regional titles are grouped when they identify the same release family; paired
versions and compilations are explicitly retained. Dates use the earliest release
year where the day/month is disputed. A feature absent from a source is unknown,
unless an inspected guide explicitly establishes its absence. Manuals and captured
execution remain necessary to settle exact timings, caps and interaction order.

## HH-001 — Bomber Boy / Atomic Punk / Dynablaster

Sources: specialist gameplay guide [H01] and release/gameplay overview [H02].

- Game A is an objective/maze campaign; destroy enemies and uncover a hidden exit.
- A world map permits seven initial worlds in any order; Faria unlocks afterward.
- Once a world is entered it must be cleared; completed worlds cannot be revisited.
- World gimmicks include directional wind affecting walking speed, teleporters
  connecting quadrants and lethal fire replacing ordinary hard walls.
- Before each round the player chooses a loadout from purchased/stored panels.
- Panels have different lifetimes: one-round, retained-until-death and instant.
- My Town buys/sells panels; world completion unlocks additional shop stock.
- Soft blocks destroyed and time remaining contribute currency; selling returns
  only part of the purchase price.
- Elixers act as reserve continues or equipped extra-hit protection, depending on
  where they are held. This is a distinct inventory/location interaction.
- Item families include bomb capacity, fire range, speed, remote detonation,
  soft-block pass, bomb pass, timer extension, flame immunity and invincibility.
- An Exit Panel permits early completion despite surviving enemies; conditional
  hidden bonus items add score goals beyond the ordinary exit condition.
- Password progress tracks cleared worlds/currency; Japanese and international
  password formats differ.
- Game B adapts NES Bomberman, retaining its separate power-up/exit campaign and
  offering continue behavior rather than simply sharing Game A's shop rules.
- Two-cartridge link battle has Panel and Powerful presets: weak initial loadout
  with collectible upgrades versus maximum fire/bombs with no item hunting.
- Match wins are configurable. No CPU battle opponent is documented for this mode.
- The specialist guide reports regional/link compatibility with Collection.

Future design interest: an explicit panel-loadout system, shop/campaign economy,
nonlinear world graph, item lifetimes and contextual extra-life semantics.

## HH-002 — Bomber King: Scenario 2 / Blaster Master Boy

Source: specialist release/box material [H03]. This is a related-series boundary
case, developed outside the usual Hudson team and rebranded abroad.

- Overhead labyrinth exploration with foliage/obstacle bombing and laser combat.
- The western premise uses Jason without his Blaster Master vehicle.
- Light/time pressure, lanterns and several named bomb classes appear in the box
  copy; exact resource rules and bomb differences require the manual.
- Multiple environmental areas lead to mutant bosses.
- Treat this as inspiration for optional adventure packs, not evidence that the
  core Bomberman simulation should acquire Blaster Master rules by default.

## HH-003 — Bomberman GB / Wario Blast

Sources: gameplay/item/battle guide [H04] and regional overview [H05].

- The solo campaign is a succession of battles against CPU bomber opponents,
  rather than a conventional monster-clearing campaign.
- Enemy count rises across preliminary rounds, followed by a boss with its own
  attacks. Bosses are not ordinary bomb-laying CPU opponents.
- Boss victories grant permanent techniques: kick, dash, tackle, line bomb and
  Motobomber hard-block jumping.
- Dash/tackle can push and stun opposing bombers; line placement spends available
  capacity; kick has a manual stop action and may erase items in its path.
- Motobomber jumps hard blocks and is a campaign technique; it does not establish
  universal airborne immunity.
- Ordinary round pickups increase bomb count/fire range; skull ailments can
  transfer by contact. Round pickups and permanent techniques reset differently.
- Arena hazards include clockwise/counterclockwise warp pads, arrows redirecting
  kicked bombs, shorter fuses, bomb-triggered stunning gas, ice and reforming blocks.
- A full-power arena changes the starting loadout and removes ordinary item drops.
- Super Game Boy exposes local battle for two to four people; three/four players
  require a multitap. This is not Game Boy link-cable multiplayer.
- Battle offers eight campaign-derived arenas and a techniques toggle, excluding
  the campaign motorcycle from that toggle's shared technique set.
- International Wario selection changes player/opponent presentation and story.
  Password reversal maps between Bomberman and Wario paths.
- Super Game Boy adds palette/border/audio enhancements; these are presentation
  capabilities distinct from the battle rules.
- Hidden passwords expose powered-up play and a survival-style opponent count mode.

## HH-004 — Bomberman GB 2 / international Bomberman GB

Sources: specialist game and control guide [H06], release overview [H07].

- Indy Bomber pursues the Ring of Wishes through an enemy-filled temple.
- Each area offers Mode A and Mode B; the latter adds ordered kills or other
  stage-specific objectives rather than merely changing the enemy palette.
- Lesson Mode samples the first stage of an area's objectives without upgrades.
- Campaign bosses award kick, dash, line bomb, tackle, motorcycle and later
  full-power support; precise award order is edition-specific source data.
- Campaign tackle stuns monsters; battle tackle pushes/stuns bombers, so the same
  technique name has role-specific behavior.
- Motobomber can jump a hard block and absorbs a hit, then returns according to
  campaign life/world transitions.
- Select can intentionally retry a stage by killing the player.
- Standard pickups are capacity/fire/extra life; skulls appear in battle.
- Battle permits CPU participants, configurable wins and timer including no limit.
- Six arenas combine different technique loadouts with arrows, linked shrines,
  slowing gas from bombed cracks, darkness and a central oil-can light source.
- A full-power battle arena uses skull-only block drops.
- Super Game Boy supports multiple human controllers with multitap; a password
  also opens battle on the handheld with CPU opponents. The specialist explicitly
  says there is no link-up play, correcting ambiguous database wording.
- Passwords normally resume at world boundaries; sound-test and full-technique
  passwords are additional presentation/practice features.

## HH-005 and HH-013 — Collection and Selection

Source: specialist compilation/region comparison [H08].

- Collection bundles Bomber Boy, Japanese GB1 and GB2 behind a launcher.
- It gives Bomber Boy a Super Game Boy palette while retaining the launcher's
  border rather than introducing an entirely new border for that game.
- Individual game mechanics remain attached to their original component.
- Korean Selection removes GB2 and Super Game Boy support, adds GBC color and
  smoother transitions, and includes a new English GB1 translation.
- Selection's GB1 preserves its original intro, unlike Wario Blast's western story.
- Emulator compatibility observations in the source are research leads, not new
  mechanics or a reason to rewrite game rules.
- Exact Korean distribution date is unresolved in this survey: a public wiki
  search result says April 2003, while the specialist links a June press release.

## HH-006 — Bomberman GB 3

Sources: detailed specialist rules/items/challenge guide [H09], overview [H10].

- Mission objectives vary per round: enemies, key collection and block puzzles.
- Three worlds are selectable in either order, followed by another group and a
  final world; selection is a graph rather than one linear stage counter.
- Some rounds span two screens; transitions reset the timer and create a retry
  checkpoint while preserving relevant collection progress.
- Death alternately reduces fire/capacity; game-over passwords preserve purchased
  upgrades and Bomber Capsule currency.
- Boss rewards fund a shop; kick, dash, line bomb and full-power starts can be
  purchased in an order chosen by the player.
- Four motorcycle classes provide different hit capacity, hard-block jumping,
  soft-block destruction or greater speed. Crushing soft blocks may erase their
  item contents, creating a speed-versus-reward choice.
- Crystal rounds award extra lives and Bells at collection thresholds.
- Bells affect the next boss encounter: upgrade drops and a preemptive boss hit.
- Clock pickups freeze timer/enemies/traps; another pickup changes speed briefly.
- Challenge Mode combines score attack and time attack, two stages, three starting
  loadouts and selectable short/long timers.
- Challenge grading accounts for time, destroyed blocks and defeated enemies;
  hurry blocks progressively reduce available space before a boss fight.
- A hidden boss-rush password is separate from the normal campaign.
- This installment has no multiplayer battle; that absence is explicit in [H09].

Date caution: the fetched encyclopedia has both December 2 and December 20 in
different fields; the specialist gives December 20. The inventory therefore uses
1996 rather than treating one conflicting day as settled.

## HH-007 — Pocket Bomberman

Sources: specialist rules/items [H11], release overview [H12].

- Side-view platforming retains the destroy-all-enemies-to-open-exit objective.
- Bombs float where placed and act as temporary platforms; upward routing is a
  core interaction rather than an incidental graphical difference.
- Normal movement includes a full jump and a lower short hop.
- Floating platforms, water jets, spikes and hidden hazards complicate traversal.
- Five themed worlds have regular levels and a boss awarding a Power Stone.
- Items include capacity/fire/speed, remote, piercing flames, maximum fire, heart,
  invincibility, time/enemy freeze, speed down and temporary repeated air jumps.
- Some upgrades persist until game over, others only until a lost life or stage
  completion. The air-jump pickup also changes jump properties.
- Jump Game forces continuous bouncing and grades ascent/score; the player still
  steers and places bombs, with separate remote controls.
- Jump Game has its own difficulty courses, sector bosses and scoring conditions.
- Level passwords, powered-up passwords and a boss rush support repeat practice.
- Japanese GB Kiss support is a separate cartridge/data-exchange feature.
- No battle or multiplayer mode is documented; [H11] explicitly identifies this.

## HH-008 — Bomberman Quest

Sources: gameplay/battle guide [H13], extensive equipment guide [H14].

- Four exploration zones plus Peace Town replace traditional self-contained grids.
- Health, monster recapture, NPC interactions, dungeons and equipment-gated paths
  make this an action adventure; bosses increase maximum health.
- Individual screens usually isolate one monster/arena. First defeat awards its
  item; repeat defeat can award healing. Returning to town resets monsters.
- A monster list records capture progress; full capture opens the true finale.
- Inventory separates active weapons assigned to A/B, passive equipment,
  consumables and crafting components.
- Crafting consumes an Explosive Core and another ingredient. Some ingredients
  have multiple possible bomb outcomes; the source reports duplicate outcomes.
- Fire upgrades affect cross-blast distance but circular-blast duration: one
  stat icon does not imply identical behavior across bomb shapes.
- Circular explosions can damage airborne enemies, adding a target-layer rule.
- Battle imports the players' campaign equipment from two cartridges over link.
- Match length, wins and whether loadouts can change between rounds are options.
- Six battle arenas and cosmetic transformations driven by wins/losses add
  persistent multiplayer identity without necessarily changing combat stats.

Other active tools: bomb-pushing stick, dancing interaction, digging shovel,
arrow-only shield, punching glove, limited-duration wings, jump shoes, held dash,
power glove for bombs/stunned monsters, hammer, remote trigger and tackle belt.
These support more than combat: statue activation, nail pounding, gap crossing,
hidden-object recovery and switches.

Passive equipment capabilities include damage reduction, status resistance,
teleport-on-hit, resistance to wind/throwing, running speed, bomb kick, lava/spike
protection, hidden-item vision, rock pushing, underwater travel, falling-rock/stun
protection, dark-area visibility, maximum fire and final-boss-specific protection.
Consumables include temporary immunity, trap freeze and healing; a reusable flute
returns to town. Trading cards unlock another bomb rather than serving only as
gallery art. Names/timings in the source remain documentary until captured.

## HH-009 — Bomberman Max versions

Sources: release/gameplay overview [H15], incomplete specialist edition registry
[H16], Ain database description [H17].

- Blue/Hikari uses Bomberman; Red/Yami uses Max, introducing a paired protagonist.
- Objective-based overhead stages have alternate exits and version-exclusive
  portions; the two games contain different collectible Charaboms.
- Infrared exchanges unlock otherwise unavailable stage content through the
  Pitch Area. Do not describe this as ordinary link-cable arena battle.
- Charabom collection, feeding, battles and merging add a companion metagame.
- The Max 2 guide explicitly contrasts the original: its Charaboms do not grant
  the same in-stage special abilities introduced in the sequel [H21].
- Infrared interaction also supplies minigame/bonus-unlock hooks; exact signal and
  version requirements need a pinned cartridge/manual experiment.
- There is no traditional bomber-versus-bomber battle according to the inspected
  gameplay accounts; companion battles are a different feature.
- Ain is a promotional Blue-family variant. A database says it has unlockables
  without the second version, but distribution, advertising and content deltas
  need direct comparison; don't turn this into a measured all-content claim.
- Complete Data is retained as an edition lead, not a demonstrated separate
  executable with a verified unique feature list.

## HH-010 — Bomberman Tournament / Bomberman Story

Sources: specialist campaign, battle, items and Karabon guides [H18][H19].

- An overworld connects towns, caves, shops, bases and puzzle gates.
- Story progression involves rescuing Max and confronting the Dastardly Bombers.
- Karabons provide selected or passive abilities, battle stats and elemental types.
- Abilities include town warping, projectile shield, special-block pushing,
  hidden-object sight, enemy/trap slowing, swimming, fire-range changes, kicking,
  bomb lifting/throwing, darkness lighting, gap bridges, dash, block pass and remote.
- Some companions serve as plot keys: calming weather, opening boss doors,
  revealing buried paths or a base. Fusion creates required new companions.
- Companion battle chooses ordered strategies using attack, defense and special;
  elemental weakness/resistance changes damage, including immunity.
- Feeding panels improve attack/defense/special stats and progression.
- Bomb crafting yields normal, mine, aqua, directly controlled, charge and
  soft-block-piercing bombs; not every bomb is merely a flame-color change.
- General inventory includes healing, armor, shoes, quest items and dungeon tools.
- Campaign and classic battle are separate: battle supports CPU play or up to four
  linked players, including single-cartridge boot.
- Battle offers configurable wins/time, starting-position shuffle, skull
  persistence/destruction and revenge from arena edges.
- Hurry blocks close the arena in a defined pattern; bombs thrown onto a player's
  head can remove an item; defeated players scatter their inventory.
- An optional winner's fishing minigame selects a reward for subsequent play.
- Eight battle arenas have distinct layouts/gimmicks; exact geometry is not
  reconstructed here.

## HH-011 — Bomberman Max 2 paired versions

Sources: detailed companion FAQ [H21], contemporary review [H22], identities [H20].

- Shrinking the heroes supplies garden/library/laboratory-scale themed worlds.
- Missions include enemy elimination, all-block destruction, fan disabling,
  selected-enemy kills while sparing others and Charabom food collection.
- Multiple exit portals create branching routes; versions differ in stage access.
- Pitching between versions unlocks otherwise unavailable stages.
- Charaboms now supply in-stage powers; switching does not erase placed bombs,
  enabling deliberate cross-companion combinations.
- Active powers share buttons with remote detonation, so equipping certain
  companions disables or replaces that action.
- Companions have HP/level, attack, defense, special and agility plus elemental
  advantages. Enemy/linked battles and fusion are separate from ordinary bombing.
- Fusion can be performed locally or with another player; contribution rules,
  retained parents and one-use fusion potential matter to completion.
- A loss in linked companion battle can transfer the defeated companion.
- Classic arena bombing multiplayer is explicitly absent in [H22].

## HH-012 — Bomberman Jetters: Legend of Bomberman

Sources: release/story registry [H23], detailed walkthrough/mechanics guide [H24].

- Mighty, rather than the console game's Shiro alone, leads a top-down quest.
- Towns, shops, multi-room dungeons, hidden cracked-wall passages, warp points,
  side quests and NPC trade chains support exploration.
- Doors may require walking on a switch, bombing it, weighting it with a bomb,
  defeating enemies or destroying blocks.
- Bomb synthesis has elemental world effects: ice freezes water routes; normal
  bombs melt them; water affects burning obstacles and plants; light reveals
  particular objects/enemies.
- The guide describes save-at-location/zone-state behavior and quicker selection
  of bombs and companions while paused; verify exact persistence independently.
- Companions evolve through food. Fruit value depends on the current evolution
  tier, giving collection a diminishing-return progression system.
- Companion families provide warping, increasingly mobile/all-direction shield,
  stronger pushing, faster dash, longer invisibility, faster/slower fuses with fire
  increases and remote detonation. Evolution changes mechanics, not only sprites.
- Bomb kick/manual stop, glove carry/throw and permanent capacity/fire/shoe upgrades
  appear separately from the companion system.
- Healing, antidotes, armor, synthesis materials and collectible album cards add
  non-combat inventory loops.
- Five battle rules are described: Classic survival; Balloon deposit scoring;
  Nugedashi key/hidden-door escape; Kawashite survival amid ceiling-dropped bombs;
  Paint ownership of floor tiles.
- Battle supports CPUs, teams, difficulty, time/win targets, random or selected
  sudden death, poison-pack destruction, win bonus and ordinary/super revenge.
- Item selection and per-player HP handicap provide independent configuration.
- Louie eggs supply mounts with bomb punch, line placement, dash or jump; the
  guide leaves the gray Louie's power uncertain, which remains an explicit gap.
- A blank-face item supplies temporary invisibility in battle.

The guide reports that armor appears not to reduce damage. This is an author's
bug observation, not a verified invariant or a defect to reproduce without a
controlled original run. Its Japanese translations and exact numerical claims
also retain that author's acknowledged language limitations.

## HH-014 — Bomberman Jetters: Game Collection

Source: contemporary controls/menu/items FAQ [H25].

- Four main entries: Pinball, Battle Game, Minigame Collection and Gallery.
- Pinball combines team rescue and high score, separate flippers, table nudge,
  pause and battery-saving sleep mode.
- Battle has one-player CPU play, multi-cartridge link and single-cartridge boot
  for up to four participants. Single-cart restricts selectable characters,
  special moves and the available arenas.
- Each CPU seat has a difficulty setting; seats can be disabled.
- Teams include a brief contact-rescue window for a teammate hit by an explosion.
- Options cover wins, timer/infinite, shuffled spawns, sudden-death behavior,
  destructible skulls, Miso Bomb and Super Miso Bomb comeback.
- Item Select controls per-item amounts under a total budget; Handicap gives each
  player an initial multi-slot loadout.
- Stages: Standard; Band Fire with changing blast-reflection crystals; Bad Weather
  with wind and slowing rain puddles; Boi-oi-oing Ring ropes with rebound dash,
  item stealing and bouncing bombs; Water Bomb moving rings that carry bombs;
  Transformation Cos-play character-changing panels; Bomb House ledges/stairs;
  Secret Block intermittently invisible ice; Dangerous Bomb hard-block penetration.
- Pickup families include fire/full fire, capacity, speed/up/down, kick, bouncing
  bombs, line placement, glove, charge glove, invisibility, heart and contagious skull.
- Super Power Glove converts a held bomb into an area bomb and has consumption rules.
- Winner's Miso Bomb Get is an intermatch aim/throw minigame that awards the hit
  player's item for the following match; it has its own controls.
- Minigames borrow/adapt Land-style attractions; their complete list is not present
  in the fetched FAQ and remains an inventory gap.
- The gallery has unlockable production art tied to minigame/pinball achievements;
  title/gallery music and character voices are separate presentation features.

## HH-015 and HH-018 — GBA NES ports/compilation

Sources: Classic NES release database [H26], compilation databases [H32][H33].

- Classic NES/Famicom Mini carries the 1985 game's timed maze clearing, bomb/fire
  upgrades, special items, enemy behavior and password progression into a GBA port.
- Hudson Best Collection Vol. 1 packages the NES original and Bomberman II.
- A launcher, save/control/display adaptation and platform-specific behavior need
  a separate wrapper audit; neither edition automatically supplies every original
  hardware multiplayer topology.
- Treat the component games' mechanics as references to their original catalogue
  entries, avoiding artificial invention of new bomb mechanics for a rerelease.

## HH-016 — N-Gage Bomberman

Sources: specialist identity [H27], contemporary hands-on review [H28].

- Overhead block/enemy clearing leads through scrolling subareas and themed worlds.
- Environmental interactions include moving structures, molten-rock hazards and
  water drainage; a rideable creature is reported alongside standard upgrades.
- Two-person Bluetooth battle is separate from the solo story.
- Three difficulty levels, language choice, volume and high-score statistics are
  documented; the save system has one frequently overwritten slot and requires
  explicit save/exit behavior in the review's account.

## HH-017 — Bomberman DS (2005)

Sources: specialist solo/item rules [H29], contemporary review [H30], identity [H31].

- Ten worlds of traditional enemy-clearing stages include boss encounters and
  invincible bonus rounds that reward clearing a timed enemy set.
- Collected items enter touch-screen storage, allowing later activation and
  recovery after losing equipped powers. Stored and active state are distinct.
- Continue resumes at designated progress points rather than guaranteeing exact
  mid-stage restoration.
- Eight battle seats support human/CPU mixes, teams and starting handicaps.
- Single-card Download Play lets visitors participate without their own copy.
- Dual-screen arenas use tunnels or trapdoors; compact arenas use one screen.
- Crown mode replaces survival with a race to a remote objective.
- Arena variants include full power, large item caches, roaming campaign enemies,
  bomb cannons, shields and open layouts without permanent hiding blocks.
- Defeated players flick revenge bombs onto the field using the stylus; Super
  Revenge restores a player after a successful elimination.
- Microphone presets activate placement, shield or remote detonation by sound.
- Locator arrows/player-name display address crowded-field readability.
- Different button actions support kick stop, punch, throw, line and shield.

Item behavior recorded in [H29]: capacity/fire/speed increases and decreases;
maximum fire; remote; piercing; a maximum-fire first bomb; hidden first-bomb mine;
rubber bounce; Dangerous Bomb's fire-dependent footprint; kick/punch/line/glove;
stationary directional flame shield; timed soft-block/bomb pass; full or
flame-only immunity; heart; added time; extra life; random item and hidden-item/
exit reveal. Soft-block pass expiration while embedded is a specific hazard.

[H30] reports 29 battle presets, but this survey does not equate that preset count
with 29 unique objectives or a fully verified arena list. The review also reports
that not all option types can be freely combined.

## HH-020 — Bomberman Portable / Legacy (PSP)

Sources: box/reference material [H35], detailed gameplay/item guide [H36].

- Normal Game offers ten worlds, regular enemy stages, boss stages and an Item
  Stock system accessed before stages, during play or while paused.
- Keys in designated stages gate the final world; the world map records them.
- Stockpile amounts, equipped effects and retained basic upgrades are distinct.
  Boss fights restrict when items may be equipped.
- Bonus enemy-clearing stages grant item caches under a timer with invincibility.
- Normal completion unlocks a 50-stage Classic Game based on the NES formula,
  rendered with 3D presentation; it is a separate rules/content preset.
- Three save files, reconfigurable controls, HUD stock/equipment switching and
  world revisiting support a portable session loop.
- Four-player ad hoc battle supports Game Sharing from one UMD.
- Battle customization, handicaps, hurry blocks and Super Miso Bomb appear in the
  inspected box copy; full battle-option enumeration remains open.
- Capacity/fire/speed, max fire, kick/stop, line, powerful first bomb, remote,
  piercing, bouncing, first-bomb mine and fire-dependent Dangerous Bomb coexist
  with timed bomb/block pass, flame/all immunity, item reveal, time, heart and life.
- The guide identifies kick and bomb-pass incompatibility, a meaningful interaction.
- Campaign progress unlocks additional battle maps as well as Classic Game.

## HH-022 — Bomberman Land Portable (PSP)

## HH-024 — Bomberman Land Touch! 2

Sources: Hudson press release [H40], gameplay/release overview [H43],

## HH-025 — Custom Battler Bomberman / Bomberman 2 (DS)

Sources: extensive equipment/skills FAQ [H44], release/mission overview [H45].

- A cyberspace/Grid City mission campaign uses collected equipment, experience,
  leveling and a health gauge, rather than only temporary battle pickups.
- Four equipment slots — head, body, arms and legs — share an Equip Point budget;
  leveling raises that budget, and parts can have positive and negative stats.
- Specs include speed, simultaneous bombs, fire extent, attack, guard and hack.
- Hack affects technical actions such as item searches and recovery/time-pack
  effects, adding a progression axis beyond movement and damage.
- Same-family part sets grant additional stats; specific two-part combinations
  unlock abilities. Full character sets supply further transformations.
- An SP gauge fills through destructive/combat activity and gates powerful skills.
- Mission scores, time grades, life bonus, chain-kill multipliers and completion
  ranks drive unlocks; score thresholds/bonus calculations need direct capture.
- Cumulative bomb/block/enemy/item milestones and per-zone ranks unlock costume
  sets, versus stages and alternate title screens.
- Battle Mode and custom battle are separate from campaign progression; whether
  a particular equipment effect applies online must be verified per mode.
- DS Rumble Pak support is an optional feedback capability [H45].
